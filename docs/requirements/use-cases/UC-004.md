# Use Case UC-004: Create an account and assign a role

> **REQ-ID**: UC-004
> **Version**: 1.0
> **Preceding Document**: [REQUIREMENTS-SPEC.md](../REQUIREMENTS-SPEC.md)

> **This use case is what makes UC-001 possible for anyone but the PM.** Without it, the bottleneck simply moves from "only the PM can edit code" to "only the PM has an account."

---

## 1. Scenario Overview

| Item | Description |
|------|------|
| **Primary User** | `super admin` (the PM) |
| **Secondary** | The new `admin` — a non-technical colleague |
| **Preconditions** | Signed in as `super admin`; the colleague has a Google work account |
| **Trigger** | A colleague needs to maintain documentation |
| **Outcome (Success)** | The colleague signs in with Google and can edit content at their assigned role |
| **Outcome (Failure)** | No account created; the attempt is logged |
| **Related FR** | FR-AUTH-001, 002, 003, 004, 005, 006, 007, 008 · FR-OPS-002 |
| **Related NFR** | NFR-SEC-AUTH, NFR-SEC-PII, NFR-SEC-SESSION, NFR-OPS-AUDIT |
| **Related BR** | BR-015, BR-016, BR-017, BR-018, BR-019, BR-020 |

> **What this replaces**: a single shared username and password held in environment variables (`SITE_USERNAME` / `SITE_PASSWORD`), producing one HMAC cookie. There is no user table, no per-person identity, and no role — access is binary. Everything below is new.

## 2. Main Flow

| Step | Actor | Action | System Response |
|------|------|------|------------|
| 1 | `super admin` | Opens **Users & roles** | Account list with role and status (Active / Deactivated) |
| 2 | `super admin` | Clicks **Add account**, enters the colleague's Google address | Address validated |
| 3 | `super admin` | Assigns a role from `user` / `admin` / `super admin` | Role recorded (**FR-AUTH-004**) |
| 4 | System | — | Account created; audit entry written (**NFR-OPS-AUDIT**) |
| 5 | New `admin` | Visits the site, clicks Sign in with Google | Google OAuth flow (**FR-AUTH-001**) |
| 6 | System | — | Identity matched to the account; session issued. **No password is stored anywhere** (**FR-AUTH-002, NFR-SEC-AUTH**) |
| 7 | New `admin` | — | Sees the admin console at their granted role; may now perform UC-001 |
| 8 | New `admin` | Signs out | Session invalidated (**FR-AUTH-007**) |

## 3. Alternative Flow

### 3.1 A-1: Change an existing role
- Action: `super admin` changes a role from `user` to `admin`
- Outcome: takes effect on the next request; change is audited

### 3.2 A-2: Deactivate a departing colleague (**FR-AUTH-003**)
- Action: `super admin` deactivates the account
- Outcome: sign-in refused from that point on. The account is **never hard-deleted** — pages they authored keep their attribution and the audit trail stays intact

### 3.3 A-3: Assign a read-only role
- Action: assign `user` rather than `admin`
- Outcome: the colleague reads internal pages but cannot reach the editor (**FR-AUTH-005**)

## 4. Exception Flow

### 4.1 E-1: Sign-in without an account
- Occurs at Step 5. Action: access refused; attempt logged (**FR-OPS-002**). Outcome: a valid Google account alone grants nothing — the account must be provisioned first.

### 4.2 E-2: An `admin` attempts account management (**FR-AUTH-008**)
- Action: refused **server-side**, not merely hidden in the UI; the attempt is logged
- Outcome: the `admin` / `super admin` boundary holds even against a direct URL. **Requires a negative test, not just a UI check.**

### 4.3 E-3: Deactivated account attempts sign-in
- Action: refused with a neutral message; logged. Outcome: no information leaked about account state.

### 4.4 E-4: Last `super admin` deactivated
- Action: refused — the system must always retain at least one `super admin`
- Outcome: administrative lockout is impossible

### 4.5 E-5: Google OAuth unavailable
- Action: sign-in fails with a clear message; nobody can sign in until it recovers
- Outcome: **accepted single point of failure.** No local password fallback exists by design (FR-AUTH-002) — noted as a deliberate trade, since a fallback would reintroduce credential handling

## 5. Non-Functional Requirements Mapping

| NFR-ID | Application |
|--------|------|
| NFR-SEC-AUTH | Steps 5–6: Google OIDC; no password storage, no reset flow, no lockout logic |
| NFR-SEC-PII | Account name and email are personal data — restricted by role, never exposed to `guest` |
| NFR-SEC-SESSION | Step 6 cookie is `httpOnly`, `secure`, `sameSite`, with **one** defined TTL. *Blocked on AMB-006 — the AS-IS contradiction between `lib/auth.ts:9` (1 day) and `CLAUDE.md:172` (30 days)* |
| NFR-OPS-AUDIT | Steps 3, 4 and every role change retained ≥ 1 year, append-only |
